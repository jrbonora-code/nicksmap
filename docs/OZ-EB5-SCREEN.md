# OZ / EB-5 rural screen

An offline preparer that answers four questions from official public sources, with no paid provider, no database, no new dependency and no PostGIS:

1. Which designated Opportunity Zone tracts are plausibly EB-5 rural? (national tract table, which also carries what the IRS says about the separate 2027 nomination round)
2. Is this specific address or coordinate inside a designated OZ and EB-5 rural? (site test)
3. Which cadastral parcels sit inside those tracts, and which of them pass the point test? (parcel pull, Montana, North Carolina and Wisconsin)
4. What subsidized multifamily already exists in those tracts, with what units, programs and expiration dates? (asset join)

Script: `scripts/oz-eb5-screen.mjs`. It is never invoked by the application, at startup or on a schedule. Outputs and raw downloads stay under the Git-ignored `.local/oz-eb5-screen/` directory. This document replaces the September 2026 planning cycle; those documents are archived locally under `.local/handoff-history/oz-eb5-planning-20260915/` and are not pending assignments.

## Data hierarchy

Everything this work produces sits in one of five layers, each keyed to the layer above. The spine is the 2010 census tract GEOID, the vintage of the 2018 designations; every 2020-vintage source is bridged to it through the Census relationship file, and every row names its source and retrieval time. New sources join an existing layer or they are not added.

| Layer | Key | Holds | Built | Planned additions |

A tract class is a screen and the statute tests the site. Missing inputs stay unknown. The OZ 2.0 rural definition and the EB-5 rural definition are different statutes and never substitute for each other. The Git-ignored data area carries a README describing its folders: products at the root, `raw/` caches with receipts (including `raw/assets/<source>/`), `import/` for the application bundle, `tools/`, and `runs/` for logs, verification scripts and comparison snapshots.

## The rule

EB-5 rural area, INA 203(b)(5)(D)(ii) as amended by the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022: any area other than an area within a metropolitan statistical area designated by OMB, or within the outer boundary of any city or town having a population of 20,000 or more based on the most recent decennial census. Two inputs, both public: OMB metropolitan membership (by county) and 2020 city or town population. Micropolitan areas do not exclude. The test is geographic and applies to the project site, so a tract can only be screened; a site can be tested.

This is not the Opportunity Zone rural definition used for the 2027 nomination round (city or town over 50,000 plus adjacent urbanized area). The two must never be substituted for one another.

## Running it

```
node scripts/oz-eb5-screen.mjs tracts                      # national table; about seven minutes with the downloads cached
node scripts/oz-eb5-screen.mjs sites <input.csv>           # input needs id + latitude/longitude, or id + address
node scripts/oz-eb5-screen.mjs parcels <MT|NC|WI>          # cadastral parcels inside that state's rural_clear tracts
node scripts/oz-eb5-screen.mjs assets                      # subsidized multifamily joined to the tract table
```

Node 24, from the repository root. The site input accepts the audited shortlist CSV directly (columns `Parcel_key`, `Latitude`, `Longitude`) or any CSV with `id`, `lat`/`latitude`, `lon`/`longitude` or `address` headers. A row must carry both a finite in-range latitude and longitude, or an address; a row with only one coordinate half and no address is `unknown` with the reason on the row, and an input with no data rows fails before any download.

Outputs of the tract mode: `oz-eb5-tracts.csv`, `oz-eb5-markets.csv`, `oz-eb5-tracts.xlsx` (sheets Tracts, **Markets**, Summary, Sources) and `manifest.json`. For sites: `<input>-eb5-oz-sites.csv`, `.xlsx` (sheets Sites, Sources) and a manifest. For parcels: `<STATE>-oz-eb5-parcels.csv`, `.xlsx` (sheets Parcels, Tracts, Sources) and a manifest. For assets: `oz-eb5-assets.csv`, `.xlsx` (sheets Assets, Summary, Sources) and `oz-eb5-assets.manifest.json`. The XLSX writer uses a constant 2020-01-01 00:00:00 archive timestamp instead of the wall clock, so identical sheet data produces identical archive bytes. The CSV outputs are byte-identical between runs; the XLSX is byte-identical except for its Sources sheet, which records the run's start time (see Verification performed).

## Tract classes

The tract mode joins on IDs only. MSA membership comes from the tract's county in the OMB list. The 20,000 test comes from IIUSA's per-tract city or town observation, applied to the 2010 tract's 2020 successors with land-area shares from the relationship file. Connecticut is the exception: OMB 2023 keys on planning regions, so each Connecticut tract is resolved at one interior point of its HUD polygon through the geocoder.

### The 0.05% sliver filter and `dropped_successors`

A 2010 tract usually maps onto its 2020 successors with one dominant part and, where a county or tract line moved a few metres, one or more parts holding a thousandth of the land. Those tiny parts carry a full IIUSA observation, including a city name and an MSA, and letting them into the place test would make a tract "not rural" because a 0.001% corner of it touches a city. Successors at or below a **0.0005 land share (0.05%)** are therefore excluded from the place test. The filter is recorded, not silent:

- `dropped_successors` names every excluded part as `<2020 GEOID> (<share>%; <IIUSA place or "no 20k place" or "no IIUSA row">; <IIUSA MSA or "no MSA">)`, so a reader can see what was set aside and decide otherwise. On the run below, 1,605 of the 8,764 tracts have at least one dropped part; all 1,605 are splits.
- `bridge_type` is computed on the relationship file **as published**, before the filter, so a 99.9% / 0.1% split is reported as a split rather than as `same_id`.
- The metro-successor `notes` warning is also computed before the filter, because a sliver successor inside an MSA is exactly the county-line case the note exists to flag.
- `place_20k_successor_land_pct` (formerly `place_20k_land_pct`) is the share of 2010 tract land mapping to filtered successors associated with a 20,000+ place.
- The manifest records `sliver_filter_share` and `tracts_with_dropped_successors`.
- A `rural_clear` reason never claims "any of its 2020 successors" when parts were dropped; it says "for the tract and its 2020 successors above the 0.05% land-share filter; N smaller parts listed in dropped_successors".

### The New England town test

IIUSA reports a city or town per tract, which does not answer whether a Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts or Rhode Island tract sits inside a *town* — the governmental unit in those states. Connecticut already goes through the interior-point path. For every tract in the other five that would otherwise classify `rural_clear`, the script takes the interior point of the HUD polygon and calls the geocoder at `vintage=Census2020_Current&layers=all`:

- `County Subdivisions` returns a town with POP100 of 20,000 or more: `rural_interpretation_dependent`, reason naming the town, its population and the fact that one interior point was tested.
- Below 20,000: stays `rural_clear`, with the note "New England town at interior point: `<name>` (`<pop>`); tract may span other towns".
- POP100 missing: `rural_interpretation_dependent`, reason "town population not returned".

The basis of each test is written to the `town_basis` column.

| Class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| rural_clear | County outside every 2023 metropolitan area, and every IIUSA observation for the tract and its 2020 successors above the 0.05% land-share filter reports no city or town of 20,000 or more; in ME, NH, VT, MA and RI the 2020 town at one interior point is also below 20,000. A site test is still required at parcel level. |
| rural_interpretation_dependent | Outside every MSA, but a 20,000+ census designated place, a 20,000+ consolidated city-county or balance-of-county entry, a partial 20,000+ place overlap after a 2010 to 2020 split, unobserved remainder, or the Connecticut and New England town question applies. Resolve at the site. |
| not_rural_place_per_iiusa | Outside every MSA, but IIUSA associates the tract with an incorporated place of 20,000 or more. Land outside that boundary may still be rural; only a site test can say. |
| not_rural_msa | County is in a 2023 metropolitan statistical area. The whole tract fails regardless of place. |
| unknown | Outside every MSA, but no IIUSA observation exists for the tract or its successors: Puerto Rico and the island territories. The place test was not performed. |

### 2027-round columns

The tract table also carries what the IRS says about the **2027 nomination round**, which is a different list on a different vintage from the 2018 designations this table is built on. Five columns, appended after `notes` so every pre-existing column keeps its position:

| Column | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `oz2027_eligible_land_pct` | Share of the 2010 tract's land that maps to 2020 successors appearing on the Rev. Proc. 2026-14 appendix |
| `oz2027_status` | `fully_eligible` at 99% or more, `partially_eligible` above 0 and below 99%, `not_eligible` at 0, `unknown` when the tract has no bridge to 2020 tracts |
| `oz2027_rural_land_pct` | Share of the 2010 tract's land mapping to successors the appendix flags rural |
| `oz2027_rural_flag` | `rural` when every eligible successor is flagged rural, `non_rural` when none is, `mixed` when they disagree, `not_eligible` when no successor is on the appendix, `unknown` with no bridge |
| `oz2027_basis` | One sentence naming the appendix, its 2020 vintage, the bridge, and that the flag is the OZ 2.0 rural definition and not EB-5 rural |

Both shares are computed on the relationship file **as published**, using the same `allParts` that feeds `dropped_successors` and before the 0.05% sliver filter: that filter exists for the 20,000 place test and has nothing to say about whether a successor appears on an IRS eligibility list. Because the two shares share one denominator, `oz2027_rural_flag` compares them directly rather than against an invented threshold.

**The two rural definitions are not interchangeable.** The appendix rural flag is OZ 2.0: a city or town over 50,000 plus the adjacent urbanized area. EB-5 rural is outside every OMB metropolitan statistical area and outside any city or town of 20,000 or more. A tract can be OZ-rural and not EB-5 rural, or the reverse. `eb5_rural_class` is never derived from these columns and these columns are never derived from it.

Also, eligibility is not designation. A tract on the appendix has been made eligible for a governor to nominate in the 2027 round; nothing here says it was nominated or certified.

A sliver successor on a list therefore reads `partial` at `0%`, which is the truthful answer rather than `no`.

**The DDA metropolitan leg is not implemented.** DDAs are not tract-keyed. Non-metropolitan DDAs are designated along county boundaries and the layer carries the county FIPS inside `DDA_CODE` as `NCNTY<FIPS>N<FIPS>`; metropolitan DDAs are designated by ZIP Code Tabulation Area and carry `ZCTA5` with no county at all. Only the county leg is joined here. A metropolitan tract reads `metro_zcta_not_evaluated`, never `no`, because answering it needs a tract-to-ZCTA crosswalk this pass does not add and a metropolitan tract must never be upgraded from the county list. Connecticut goes through the same planning-region key the OMB leg already uses: HUD designates Northeastern Connecticut (09150) and Northwest Hills (09160) as non-metropolitan DDAs, and both designated Connecticut tracts outside a metropolitan area sit in them.

**Connecticut cannot be answered for QCT.** HUD codes all 164 of its 2026 Connecticut QCTs on the 2022 planning regions (09110 to 09190), while the Census 2010-to-2020 relationship file still keys Connecticut on the 2020 counties 09001 to 09015. No Connecticut successor can be matched, so all 72 designated Connecticut tracts read `unknown` rather than a bridged `no` that the sources do not support. The script detects this from the data — a state whose QCT GEOIDs are all absent from the relationship file — and records it in `manifest.lihtc_qct_2026.unreachable_states`; Connecticut is the only such state on this run and no state is partly unreachable.

**LIC eligibility is not an allocation.** An NMTC-eligible tract is one a community development entity may serve; nothing here says an allocation was awarded, a qualified equity investment was made or a project was financed. The same holds for QCT and DDA: both are eligibility for the 30% boost to LIHTC qualified basis, not a credit award, an allocation or a funded project.

### ACS demographics

The same three facts about a tract at two dates, appended after `nmtc_basis` so every pre-existing column keeps its position: poverty rate, median family income and population, **at designation** and **latest**. They are sample estimates that describe the tract. **None of them is an EB-5 input**: `eb5_rural_class` is never derived from them and they are never derived from it, and none of them is a current condition, a market study or a statement about any site.

At designation means the 2011-2015 American Community Survey, the vintage in force when the 2018 designations were made, read from the CDFI Fund's own 2011-2015 low-income-community file. That file is on 2010 tracts, so it joins on the tract's own GEOID with no bridge, and the test it applies (poverty rate 20% or more, or median family income 80% or less of the statewide or metropolitan benchmark) is the test that defined Opportunity Zone eligibility, so its flag is the designation-era eligibility reading. Latest means the ACS 2020-2024 5-year release, the most recent at the time of the run, read from the Census Bureau's table-based summary files for B01003, B17001 and B19113. Those are on 2020 tracts and reach a 2010 tract only through the relationship file, and here the rule is stricter than for a flag: a flag can be expressed as a land share, a population cannot. A count is carried only when every 2020 successor lies wholly inside the 2010 tract (at least 99.9% of the successor's own land is in the part), so the sum is the tract's own figure and the poverty rate is the exact aggregate. A median is never combined, so it is carried only from a single successor. A single successor that extends beyond the tract gives that wider tract's rate and median, named as such, and no count. Nothing is apportioned by land share, because apportioning a sample estimate invents a figure the source does not publish.

| Column | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `acs2015_poverty_rate` | The file's poverty rate for the tract, 2011-2015 ACS, percent |
| `acs2015_mfi_ratio_pct` | The file's median family income as a percent of its statewide or metropolitan benchmark; already a percent in this file, which the loader checks (national median 90%) rather than assumes, because the 2016-2020 file writes the same column as a ratio |
| `acs2015_poverty_universe_pop` | The file's population for whom poverty status is determined; not total population, and named so |
| `acs2015_lic_eligible` | `yes` / `no` from the file's own test, `unknown` with no row |
| `acs2015_basis` | Source, vintage, the direct join, the test, what the population is, which figures the file reports as `N/A`, and that these are decade-old sample estimates |
| `acs2024_coverage` | `whole` (every successor wholly inside), `containing_tract` (one successor extends beyond the tract), `partial` (several successors, at least one extends beyond), `unknown` (no bridge, a state the tables cannot reach, or a successor with no table row) |
| `acs2024_population` | B01003 total population, exact sum over the successors, `whole` coverage only |
| `acs2024_poverty_universe_pop` | B17001 population for whom poverty status is determined, exact sum, `whole` only |

## Site classes

The site mode calls the geocoder three times per point (2010 tract, 2020 geographies with population, current CBSA layers) and HUD once. It reports the 2010 tract and CDFI membership, the HUD polygon at the point and whether the two agree, the OMB status of the county or Connecticut planning region, the geocoder's own CBSA read as a cross-check, the incorporated place or CDP with 2020 population, the county subdivision with population for the New England town question, and a class: not_rural_msa, not_rural_place, rural_interpretation_dependent, rural_clear or unknown. The `oz_and_eb5_rural` column collapses that to Y, CHECK, N, N (not a designated OZ), or unknown. Every row carries the note that this is a point test: a parcel can straddle a tract, place or county line.

Three things the site mode refuses to guess. If the geocoder returns no 2010 tract, the class is `unknown` and `oz_and_eb5_rural` is `unknown`; "N (not a designated OZ)" is only written when a tract was returned and is absent from the CDFI list. If a place or CDP is returned with no `POP100`, the class is `unknown` with the reason "place population not returned by the geocoder" rather than being read as a population of zero; the same applies to the New England county-subdivision population. If a row's coordinates are incomplete or out of range and it carries no address, the class is `unknown` with the reason on the row.

The parcel mode reads `oz-eb5-tracts.csv`, takes that state's `rural_clear` designated tracts, and for each one pulls the HUD designated 2010 polygon and then every cadastral parcel that intersects it. It asserts before starting that no `rural_clear` tract is metropolitan, which is what lets the per-parcel check ask only the 20,000 city-or-town question: the MSA leg is already carried on the tract row.

**Paging is keyset, by object ID.** Each page asks for `<order_field> > <last object ID seen>` with `orderByFields` ascending and `resultRecordCount` at the service's advertised page cap; duplicates are dropped by ID and paging stops when a page adds nothing new (with a 500-page guard). This replaced `resultOffset` paging, which over-fetched on the NC and WI services even with `orderByFields` set: the server returns a different window for the same offset, so later pages repeat rows from earlier ones. The evidence is in the two abandoned runs kept beside the outputs. In `parcels-NC-offsetpaging.log`, Ashe `37009970400` reported a server count of 4,974 and fetched 9,512 rows across 5 pages; Avery `37011930302` 3,158 against 4,123; Bertie `37015960200` 3,338 against 4,549. In `parcels-WI-offsetpaging.log`, Ashland `55003950300` reported 1,257 and fetched 2,670 across 8 pages; Barron `55005000600` 3,182 against 4,393. Montana did not over-fetch under either scheme (`parcels-MT-offsetpaging.log` and `parcels-MT.log` both reconcile at 58,361 server / 58,360 fetched), so the defect is service-specific and would have gone unnoticed on a single-state run. Every tract row still carries `server_count`, `fetched_count`, `pages` and a `reconciliation` of `complete` or `incomplete`, and rows are kept either way.

**Containment.** Each parcel polygon is tested vertex by vertex against the HUD polygon with a banded even-odd point-in-polygon index built for that tract; `whole_parcel_in_oz` means every vertex is inside, `partial` means some vertices are outside but the parcel's interior point is inside, `edge_only` means the interior point is outside as well. The index is cross-checked against `@turf/boolean-point-in-polygon` on every vertex of the first 40 real parcels of the first tract; the manifest records the comparison.

**Point check.** One geocoder call per selected parcel, where selected means developable and `whole_parcel_in_oz`, ordered 1 to 200 acres first and then largest first, capped at **30 parcels per tract**. The call is `Census2020_Current` at the parcel's interior point and reads Incorporated Places, Census Designated Places and County Subdivisions with `POP100`. A place or New England town returned with no population makes `eb5_point_class` `unknown` and the verdict `CHECK`; it never falls through to `rural_clear`. `oz_and_eb5_rural` is `Y` only for a whole-parcel-in-OZ parcel whose point check returned `rural_clear`, `N` for `edge_only` or a 20,000+ incorporated place, `CHECK` for `partial` and for every unresolved point, and `tract_only` for the parcels that were never point-checked.

**Owner fields are never requested.** All three services expose owner-name fields. The `outFields` list for each service is fixed in the script and contains none of them, so no owner name is requested, cached in the raw page files, or written to an output. The manifest records `owner_name_fields_requested: false`.

**Columns.** `state`, `geoid_2010`, `tract_county_name`, `cbsa_type`, `cbsa_title`, `parcel_id`, `alt_parcel_id`, `source_object_id`, `parcel_county`, `situs_address`, `city`, `zip`, `source_acres`, `source_acres_field`, `source_acres_alt`, `source_acres_alt_field`, `geometry_acres_approx`, `acres_used`, `acres_basis`, `land_use_raw`, `land_use_note`, `improved_flag_raw`, `land_value`, `building_value`, `total_value`, `assessment_vintage`, `county_fips_source`, `developable_flag`, `developable_reason`, `oz_containment`, `parcel_vertices`, `vertices_outside_oz`, `interior_lat`, `interior_lon`, `geometry_note`, `eb5_point_class`, `place_name`, `place_pop_2020`, `place_kind`, `eb5_point_basis`, `oz_and_eb5_rural`, `tract_reconciliation`, `source_url`, `source_page`. ## Limits that stay visible

- A tract class is a screen. The statute tests the site. Never publish a tract class as a project's eligibility.
- IIUSA's city or town observation is one place per tract with an unpublished method. It is the industry reference layer, and the place leg is not independently recomputed here. The MSA leg is carried by the OMB county join, which is an independent source but not a confirmation of IIUSA on every row: where the two point different ways the row carries a note. On the September 16, 2026 run, 54 of the 8,764 rows carry that metro-successor note, meaning IIUSA places at least one 2020 successor inside an MSA while the tract's county is outside every 2023 OMB metropolitan area (50 of the 54 are rural_clear). Those are county-line or vintage differences to settle at the site. A site test is the independent check of the place leg.
- USCIS treatment of CDPs, New England towns and consolidated cities under the 20,000 test is not settled. What that means concretely: a 20,000+ CDP makes the tract `rural_interpretation_dependent`; an IIUSA entry that is a consolidated city-county, a "(balance)" entry or a merged municipality does the same rather than counting as an incorporated city of 20,000; a `rural_clear` tract in ME, NH, VT, MA or RI is tested against the 2020 town at one interior point and moves to `rural_interpretation_dependent` if that town reaches 20,000 or returns no population; Connecticut is resolved at an interior point and never left `rural_clear`. Each case is written into `class_reason`, and `town_basis` records the New England test. These are flagged, not decided.
- OMB delineations change. The 2023 bulletin is used; the geocoder cross-check will surface a later delineation when the Census Bureau adopts one. The loader refuses any List 1 row whose Metropolitan/Micropolitan column is not exactly "Metropolitan Statistical Area" or "Micropolitan Statistical Area" rather than defaulting it to micropolitan.
- The 2018 designations run to December 31, 2028. Verified September 16, 2026 against [26 U.S.C. 1400Z-1](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title26-section1400Z-1&num=0&edition=prelim): the current prelim text has no subsection (f); Pub. L. 119-21 section 70421(b)(2) redesignated the former (f) as (e) and that amendment applies only to areas designated after July 4, 2025. The struck subsection (f), which governs the 2018 round, is preserved in the section's amendment notes and reads "A designation as a qualified opportunity zone shall remain in effect for the period beginning on the date of the designation and ending at the close of the 10th calendar year beginning on or after such date of designation." The designations were certified in December 2018, so the tenth calendar year beginning on or after that date is 2028 and the designations close on December 31, 2028. The 2027 round is a separate list on 2020 boundaries: the table reports each 2010 tract's overlap with the IRS eligibility appendix in the `oz2027_*` columns, and nothing more. Eligibility for nomination is not a nomination, a certification or a designation, and no row here says a tract will be an Opportunity Zone after 2028.
- No row establishes availability, ownership, a listing, a permit, financing or a TEA designation letter. High-unemployment TEAs are a different test and are out of scope.

## In the application

The tract table is readable inside the signed-in workspace at `/research/oz-eb5`. The screen script is still the offline authority: nothing in the application runs it, reads its output directory or writes to it. The application reads one explicitly imported copy of the table.

Migration 008 (September 18, 2026, built and verified locally, not released) carries the rest of layer 1 and layer 4 into the application: the five `oz2027_*` columns, the ten incentive-flag columns and the eleven ACS demographic columns as nullable extensions of the tract table, and `oz-eb5-assets.csv` as `research_oz_eb5_assets`. The preparer (`oz-eb5-import-2`) now requires all 52 tract columns by name, reads the assets CSV and verifies it against the assets manifest's own SHA-256, refuses any column whose name suggests an owner, agent, phone or email, and refuses an asset whose class disagrees with its tract (which means the assets mode ran against a different tract table). The page shows the positive incentive readings as badges beside each tract, the ACS reading at designation against the latest, and a per-tract asset count, with every basis string one disclosure away; two switches keep only tracts with a LIHTC basis boost or with subsidized properties; an Assets tab lists the properties for a state or a selected tract, by source, with the expiring-by-2030 filter. A run imported by the earlier parser is not silently upgraded: its new cells read "not carried by this run" until a newer bundle is imported. The release contract and verification are in [RESEARCH-RELEASE.md](RESEARCH-RELEASE.md#oz--eb-5-extensions-and-assets-migration-008).

**Getting the table in.** Two explicit operator commands, in order, neither of them ever run at startup or on a schedule:

```
node scripts/prepare-oz-eb5-import.mjs [screenDir] [outDir]   # reads oz-eb5-tracts.csv + manifest.json, pulls and
                                                              # simplifies the HUD polygons, writes the bundle
node scripts/db-migrate.mjs                                   # applies 007-oz-eb5.sql
node scripts/import-oz-eb5.mjs .local/oz-eb5-screen/import/oz-eb5-import.json.gz
```

The preparer refuses to run if `oz-eb5-tracts.csv` or `list1_2023.xlsx` does not match the SHA-256 in the screen manifest, pulls the HUD designated 2010 polygon for every tract that is not `not_rural_msa` (using the geometry alias where one exists), simplifies each to a display polygon at tolerance 0.00008, carries OMB Bulletin 23-01 List 1 for every county in the file, and writes a gzip bundle with a manifest of receipts. The bundle is content addressed by the SHA-256 of its manifest, so the import is a replay when the same screen output is loaded twice: it re-points the current run rather than writing a second copy. The import runs in one transaction under an advisory lock; a failure rolls back and leaves the previously imported run live. Both the run and its rows are append-only.

**Routes.** All four require the workspace session and call the identity check before they look at a parameter, so an unauthenticated request is refused without learning which value would have been rejected.

**What the map shows and does not show** (redesign of October 16, 2026). The page opens on the national fit with one class-shaped point per non-metropolitan tract — 1,968 points: rural clear (filled circle), interpretation dependent (diamond), not rural by place (ring) and unknown (square, the 136 Puerto Rico and island tracts, which carry geometry and are now in the legend). From zoom 7 the simplified HUD 2010 polygons take over, fetched per state in view and named by run so the browser caches them. `not_rural_msa` tracts — 6,796 of the 8,764 — are not drawn because they fail the rural test on the metropolitan leg regardless of place and carry no geometry; the Layers panel lists them as "Metro, not drawn". The class checkboxes in that panel are the class filter; its incentive overlay hatches QCT, DDA or NMTC tracts. Polygons are simplified for display and are not survey boundaries; a fill is a tract screen, never a site result. List, map and drawer are linked: hovering highlights the same tract on both sides, clicking either selects it (the map flies to it, the row scrolls into view, the drawer opens), arrow keys move the selection and Escape closes it, and the URL carries the mode, filters, tract, view and map position. A point label sits inside each tract's polygon (memoised per run in the service, never outside a C-shaped or holed tract).

**The 1% badge rule.** A `partial` QCT or NMTC flag, or a 2027 eligibility, that covers under 1% of the tract's land gets no badge; the drawer reads "Sliver only" with the share. Choctaw County `01023956800` is the case that prompted it: its only 2027-eligible part is the 0.003% sliver `01023956900`. On the September 18 run the rule removes the badge from 309 of 992 partly 2027-eligible tracts, 313 of 972 partial QCT and 181 of 767 partial NMTC. `incentiveBadges` in `lib/research/oz-eb5.ts` holds it.

**Properties count buildings.** HUD-assisted, USDA and LIHTC records often describe one building: on the September 18 run the 5,554 records in non-metropolitan tracts are 4,620 distinct properties under the rule below, 691 of them described by more than one source. The list counts distinct properties by normalised address and city (`PROPERTY_KEY_SQL`, with a TypeScript twin pinned by tests) and never sums units across sources: Choctaw holds 10 records and 5 buildings. The drawer groups records by building and shows each building's units as its sources report them.

**Known text defect, fixed in the script and not yet re-imported.** `scripts/oz-eb5-screen.mjs` counted the parts listed in `dropped_successors` by splitting on "; ", which also splits inside each entry, so all 489 `class_reason` strings that cite a dropped-part count tripled it. The count now matches GEOIDs. The imported run still carries the old text until the screen is rerun, prepared and imported (operator, later); classes and every other column are unaffected.

**The site test's vintages.** One point goes to the Census Geocoder three times and to the HUD Opportunity Zones FeatureServer layer 13 once, from the server, each call with its own 15-second timeout: benchmark `Public_AR_Current`; vintage `Census2010_Current` for the 2010 tract that designation is keyed to, `Census2020_Current` for the county, incorporated place, census designated place and county subdivision with `POP100`, and `Current_Current` for the CBSA cross-check and for the Connecticut planning region that OMB 2023 keys on. The metropolitan leg is answered from the imported OMB List 1 by that current-vintage county or planning region, not from the geocoder; the geocoder's own CBSA read is reported beside it and a disagreement is noted on the result. A failed lookup is reported as `unknown` with its reason, never as a rural or non-rural finding.

**The site rules exist twice, deliberately.** `scripts/oz-eb5-screen.mjs` is a CLI entry point with top-level dispatch that exports nothing, so it cannot be imported; `classifySite` in `lib/research/oz-eb5.ts` is the same reviewed rule set written as a pure function over already-fetched layers. They are kept aligned by three things: the script stays the authority and is not edited from the application side; `tests/research/oz-eb5.test.ts` pins every boundary the two must share (the 20,000 turn at 19,999 / 20,000 / 20,001, incorporated place versus CDP versus New England town, the four paths that must stay `unknown`, the metropolitan leg winning regardless of place, and the Connecticut planning-region basis string); and any change to either copy is a change to both, checked against those tests. `scripts/prepare-oz-eb5-import.mjs` likewise duplicates the script's minimal zip and xlsx reader for the same reason.

One difference between the copies is deliberate and is not drift. Where the class is `unknown` because a population was not returned, the script's `oz_and_eb5_rural` collapse falls through to `N`; the application reports `unknown`. Calling an undecided point "not rural" asserts a finding the sources did not support, and the parcel mode already treats the same case as `CHECK`. The classes themselves are identical in both copies.

A tract row in the application is still a screen. It does not establish EB-5 eligibility, a targeted employment area designation, an approved project, ownership, availability or financeability, and a site result is a point test that does not establish a whole parcel.

Florida meeting summaries found only on aggregator or news sites are not shipped.
